\section{Classical product-feature estimator control}
\label{app:productcontrol}
This control was added after the finite-shot study to separate a
nonlinear representation change from the way that representation
is estimated. It uses the same frozen Hubregtsen circuit and ideal
Z-outcome distribution, not a new circuit family or hardware dataset.
Let $Z_q\in\{-1,1\}$ have mean $\mu_q$ at one input and parameter
state. The diagonal tail preserves the product-state computational
basis probabilities, so the four coordinates $Z_q$ are independent.

\subsection{Unbiasedness and conditional variance}
For $N$ shots in one independent half, define
$\bar Z_q=N^{-1}\sum_sZ_q^{(s)}$. Direct ZZ estimation uses
$N^{-1}\sum_sZ_q^{(s)}Z_r^{(s)}$, whereas the classical lift uses
$\bar Z_q\bar Z_r$. Independence across qubits makes both unbiased
for $\mu_q\mu_r$. Their variances are respectively
\begin{align}
 V_{\rm direct}&=(1-\mu_q^2\mu_r^2)/N,\\
 V_{\rm product}&=\frac{\mu_q^2(1-\mu_r^2)+\mu_r^2(1-\mu_q^2)}{N}
 +\frac{(1-\mu_q^2)(1-\mu_r^2)}{N^2}.
\end{align}
Their difference is
$(1-\mu_q^2)(1-\mu_r^2)(1/N-1/N^2)\ge0$.
This scalar observation alone is not sufficient for a dense metric,
because covariances between different features also matter.

The full comparison follows by conditioning. Given each qubit's
sample count in a half, its outcome sequence is uniformly permuted;
different qubit sequences remain conditionally independent. Therefore
the conditional expectation of each direct parity average equals the
product of its marginal means. Condition separately on the counts
of every input, parameter state, and independent half. The two halves
remain independent, and bilinearity gives
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb E[\widehat d^2_{\rm direct}\mid\text{marginal counts}]
 =\widehat d^2_{\rm product}.
\end{equation}
The same holds for any fixed linear contrast of distance differences.
The law of total variance proves non-increasing variance, for any
fixed metric, without changing the target signal. This is the usual
conditional-expectation variance reduction, not a quantum advantage.
It assumes factorization of the Z-outcome distribution. For instance,
a distribution supported equally on $++$ and $--$ has zero local
means and unit ZZ mean, so a product-of-means substitute is incorrect.

\subsection{Exact covariance and frozen comparison}
For a feature indexed by a nonempty qubit set $I$, put
$\widehat f_I=\prod_{q\in I}\bar Z_q$. Independence gives
\begin{equation}
 \mathbb E[\widehat f_I\widehat f_J]
 =\prod_{q\in I\cap J}\left(\mu_q^2+\frac{1-\mu_q^2}{N}\right)
  \prod_{q\in I\triangle J}\mu_q.
\end{equation}
Subtracting $\prod_{q\in I}\mu_q\prod_{q\in J}\mu_q$ gives the
complete feature covariance. Summing the independent two-input
covariances gives $S$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:splitvariance}. That variance
formula only needs independent, unbiased halves; it does not require
the feature estimator itself to be linear in outcomes.

We retain all three frozen panels, five held-out points, four target
directions, six metric arms and four budgets, with 256 replicates.
The 1,440 comparison records share data and metrics and are not
independent discoveries. No new metric or favorable contrast is
selected: the direct-parity oracle contrast is fixed for both arms.
At each state, both estimates use exactly the same bitstring counts
and total shot budget. Table~\ref{tab:productcontrol} reports the
frozen noise-aware arm; the data retain all six arms.

The product-estimator empirical/exact variance ratios have pooled
median $0.990$ and 5th--95th percentiles $[0.869,1.120]$ across
the correlated comparison records. At 4,096 shots, panel-median
empirical mean-squared-error ratios are $0.890,0.827,0.907$.
These are descriptive Monte Carlo checks; unbiasedness follows from
the factorization argument, not a significance test. Dense-state
probabilities agree with product probabilities within
$1.7\times10^{-15}$, and exact-feature products agree within
$1.8\times10^{-15}$. Direct-parity samples match the saved Linux
study within $5.6\times10^{-14}$. The new arm was run locally with
Python 3.9.6 and NumPy 1.24.4; server reproduction remains pending.
Four unit tests include exact enumeration of all 256 two-shot,
four-qubit outcome sequences and their conditional expectations.
The separate control bundle retains its pre-execution scope,
frozen-plan hash, code, per-record variances and paired samples.
