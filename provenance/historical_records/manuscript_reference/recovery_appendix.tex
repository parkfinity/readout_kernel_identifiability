\section{Metric recovery: proofs, exact evidence, and scope}
\label{app:recovery}
\subsection{Quantifiers in the metric-recovery statement}
Proposition~\ref{prop:metricrecover} concerns a fixed parameter point
with a nonconstant feature map, and quantifies over \emph{all} fixed
positive-definite metrics. To justify its linear-algebra step, from
$ab^T+ba^T=0$ and $a\ne0$, multiply by $a$ to obtain
$a(a^Tb)+b\norm{a}^2=0$. Inner product with $a$ gives $a^Tb=0$,
and hence $b=0$. A direction that changes features only by a common
translation is invisible to every metric. At a constant feature map,
however, every squared-distance first derivative vanishes even if
the directional feature derivative is nonconstant.

For the generic-metric observation in Sec.~\ref{sec:recovery}, fix
$\theta$ and consider the linear map
$v\mapsto C^TMH_v+H_v^TMC$ in finite coordinates. Each matrix entry
is linear in the independent entries of $M$. A nonzero maximal-rank
minor is thus a nonzero polynomial, whose zero set has measure zero
in the chosen open metric family. Apply this argument in full
symmetric coordinates for dense metrics or in diagonal coordinates
for diagonal metrics. A finite union of exceptional sets still has
measure zero. Positive rescaling of $M$ does not change rank, so
trace normalization is harmless. This is not a uniform guarantee at
every parameter point, a conditioning bound, or an efficient
maximum-rank algorithm. In particular, exchanging the quantifiers
``for every direction there exists a metric'' and ``there exists one
metric for every direction'' is invalid.

\subsection{Exact fixed-readout ceiling and correlation recovery}
Writing $(a,b,c,d)=(\sin\theta_0,\sin\theta_1,
\sin\theta_2,\sin\theta_3)$, the local-Z coefficient matrix, up to
an overall sign, is
\begin{equation}
 C=\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\0&b\\c&0\\0&d\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
For diagonal $M$, $G=C^TMC$ has two diagonal entries and its
Jacobian in $(a,b)$ has minor $4ab w_0w_1\ne0$ generically.
For the dense metric in Eq.~\eqref{eq:recoverymetric}, evaluate at
\begin{align}
 (\sin\theta_0,\ldots,\sin\theta_3)&=(3/5,5/13,8/17,7/25),\\
 (\cos\theta_0,\ldots,\cos\theta_3)&=(4/5,12/13,15/17,24/25).
\end{align}
The Jacobian of $(G_{00},G_{01},G_{11})$ in
$(\theta_0,\theta_1,\theta_2)$ has exact determinant
\begin{equation}
 -\frac{835727837184}{978271035584375}\ne0.
\end{equation}
Together with $A(A+1)/2=3$, this proves maximal fixed-dense-metric
rank three. The centered features themselves have generic rank four.

Before the diagonal controlled-rotation tail, the state is a product,
and that tail commutes with all Z strings. Thus $f_{qr}(x)=f_q(x)f_r(x)$
for the added ZZ features. All these features remain independent of
the four tail symbols, giving rank at most four. A saved diagonal
metric and nonzero $4\times4$ Fourier-Gram minors certify the matching
lower bound. There is also a direct analytic lower bound for any fixed
positive diagonal metric on these ten features. Put
$u=\cos x_0$ and $v=\cos x_1$. The five nonconstant data functions
$u,v,u^2,v^2,uv$, together with 1, are independent on the input
domain. Four diagonal entries of their coefficient Gram matrix are
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \beta_u&=w_a a^2+w_c c^2,&\beta_{u^2}&=w_{ac}a^2c^2,\\
 \beta_v&=w_b b^2+w_d d^2,&\beta_{v^2}&=w_{bd}b^2d^2.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here $w_{ac}$ and $w_{bd}$ are the positive weights of $Z_0Z_2$
and $Z_1Z_3$. The $(a,c)$ Jacobian block for the first pair has
determinant $4acw_{ac}(w_a a^2-w_c c^2)$, and the $(b,d)$ block
has the analogous generically nonzero determinant. Multiplying
by the four cosine derivatives converts to the angle Jacobian and
remains generically nonzero. Thus the rank is four. This argument
also shows that these two same-input-group correlations suffice
under diagonal weighting; the experiment retained all six ZZ
correlations and does not test minimal feature selection.
The two stored modular certificates independently reduce rational coefficients
at a valid rational-phase parameter point; nonzero modular minors
provide lower bounds, not upper bounds from modular zeros. Their
saved integer matrices were checked again together with the other
40 minor certificates. The upper bound follows from the exact absence
of the tail symbols, not from sampling.

The initial metric survey used 30 readout settings on these ten

The one-basis search used Pauli words of weight at most two and
joint compatible parities, not one new circuit per observable.
Its selected designs reached full source rank in all ten cases,

full sampled rank in all ten. Uniform X and uniform Y each did so
in eight cases. This strong control prevents attributing the result
to a generally superior combinatorial readout-search algorithm.

\section{Finite-shot design protocol and complete budget comparison}
\label{app:shotprotocol}
\subsection{Fixed design, common targets, and estimators}
The follow-up inventory contains three source circuits and four
readout cases, not four independent families: two-qubit, one-layer
YZ-CX/local XYZ and Kyriienko/local XYZ, and four-qubit, one-layer
Hubregtsen/local Z and Z+ZZ. Three fixed seed panels each use three
design points and five held-out points, with 16 disjoint input pairs
at each point, drawn uniformly from $(-0.95,0.95)^2$. The original
raw or arccos encoding is preserved. No labels, classification
datasets, or predictive metrics enter this experiment.

For each point, center the parameter derivatives of the reference
features by subtracting one input's derivatives. Their sampled row
space defines the target parameter subspace. Both Hubregtsen cases
use the \emph{local-Z reference space}, of dimension four, to avoid
changing the target when adding ZZ features. The component invisible
to the initial isotropic kernel is recorded separately. A canonical
orthonormal basis is obtained from the subspace projector by
projected-coordinate Gram--Schmidt, with a positive largest component.
The basis, inputs, parameters, metrics and selection indices are
stored before the server run. No test-time SVD sign or favorable
direction selection is required.

The candidate bank has the identity, evenly spaced diagonal weights
from 1 to 4, sixteen random diagonals, and sixteen random dense
positive-definite matrices. All have unit trace and condition number
at most four. Both selectors maximize the worst design-point target
rank, then the mean logarithm of the smallest retained singular value
of either $JQ$ or $W$ at 1,024 shots. The first random draw is an
untuned baseline, and every candidate's held-out diagnostics are
retained. The metric is fixed thereafter across points and budgets.
Thus noise-aware selection is an ideal-model finite-bank heuristic;
it is not trained on shot outcomes or task labels.

Let $\widehat a$ and $\widehat b$ be independent half-shot estimates
of the same feature difference $\delta$, each with covariance $S$.
The estimator $\widehat d^2=\widehat a^T M\widehat b$ obeys
\begin{align}
 \mathbb E\widehat d^2&=\delta^TM\delta,\label{eq:splitmean}\\
 \operatorname{Var}(\widehat d^2)
 &=2\delta^TMSM\delta+\Tr(MSMS).\label{eq:splitvariance}
\end{align}
To see this, write $\widehat a=\delta+e$ and
$\widehat b=\delta+e'$, with independent zero-mean errors. The two
linear terms have total variance $2\delta^TMSM\delta$; the bilinear
term has variance $\Tr(MSMS)$ and zero covariance with the linear
terms. Joint outcome covariances, including ZZ correlations, enter
$S$. Input pairs are disjoint so their estimates are independent.
Neither clipping negative distance estimates nor exponentiating
them is part of this unbiased-distance analysis.

For finite displacements $\theta\pm0.05v$ along \emph{each} frozen
target basis direction, the two state estimates use independent
draws. Their exact distance variances add. Every method receives
its own ideal matched linear contrast, proportional to the exact
pairwise signal divided by its variance and normalized to unit
Euclidean norm. This contrast is fixed before Monte Carlo sampling.
It treats methods symmetrically but uses oracle model information:
the resulting SNR is not calibrated detection power or a practical
shot-only identification algorithm. It is also distinct from the
local-limit singular-value statistic in Table~\ref{tab:shotdesign}.

\subsection{Measurement accounting and uncertainty}
Each observable is assigned to its first compatible product basis.
Within-basis covariances are preserved by joint multinomial sampling.
For requested budget $B$ and $L$ bases, each half/basis gets
$\lfloor B/(2L)\rfloor$ shots, so the actual budget is
$2L\lfloor B/(2L)\rfloor$ per input and parameter state. Local XYZ
uses three bases and hence actual budgets $252,1020,4092,16380$;
local Z and Z+ZZ use one basis and the requested budgets exactly.
For one direction and one replicate, the 16 pairs and two parameter
states require 64 times that per-input budget. Multiplication by
256 replicates and by the number of target directions gives the
simulation workload. Metrics reuse the same outcomes, and matched
Hubregtsen Z/Z+ZZ cases reuse the same ZZZZ draws. Observable count
is not substituted for basis count or total shots.

There are 60 panel/readout/probe records, not 60 independent
scientific problems. We report each panel separately and do not
interpret repeated probes, different budgets, or shared metric arms
as independent discoveries. The pooled variance-ratio percentiles
summarize calibration across correlated checks, not confidence
intervals. The largest absolute mean-bias/Monte-Carlo-SE diagnostic
is 4.07 among the many checked cells; it is not a single preselected
hypothesis test. Analytic unbiasedness is given by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:splitmean}, while empirical means, standard deviations,
and all samples are saved for inspection.

\subsection{Reproduction and what remains unproved}
The Linux full run used Python~3.8.10, NumPy~1.24.4 and
SymPy~1.13.3. A full macOS replay reproduced all 60 records and
1,658,880 saved Monte Carlo contrast values within relative tolerance
$10^{-9}$ and absolute tolerance $10^{-11}$; the maximum absolute
sample discrepancy was $1.04\times10^{-13}$. All 21 recorded package
file hashes matched, and sampled ranks were stable at the three
specified tolerances. Ten unit tests cover covariance, unbiased
variance, frozen splits, the common target, and shared Z/ZZ counts.
This is same-code cross-platform reproducibility, not independent
theoretical verification or device validation.

The preceding recovery survey also had a full Linux rerun. Its
exact certificates and rank diagnostics agreed. In its initial
finite-shot pilot, an unfrozen SVD direction reversed sign for one
YZ-CX case across platforms; this changed the allocation of random
draws to the two states, not the direction axis. Replaying the saved
server direction reproduced the statistics. The present follow-up
freezes the full direction basis and metric choices. Its symmetric
oracle contrasts are computed from exact model probabilities before
drawing shot samples, rather than fitted to the sampled outcomes.
An RNG seed alone was not treated as sufficient for reproducibility.

These ideal-model experiments neither compare supervised metric
learning nor establish predictive generalization, optimal measurement
selection, calibrated identification at fixed error, or QPU shot
savings. In particular, better retained sensitivity can coexist with
an unidentified direction, and full rank can coexist with a small
weakest sensitivity. Those distinctions, rather than a general
acceleration claim, connect the design study to the exact
identifiability analysis.
