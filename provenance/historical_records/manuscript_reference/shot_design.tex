\section{Finite-shot sensitivity and estimator controls}
\label{sec:shotdesign}
The question here is whether restored ideal dependence is well
resolved at a fixed shot budget, not whether rank alone improves
prediction. Grouped Pauli measurement and shot allocation affect
finite-sampling error, so the number of observables alone is not a
measurement-cost metric \cite{crawford2021efficient}. We fix the
measurement schedules below rather than optimize over all grouping
or shot-allocation strategies.
The follow-up fixes three seed panels, each with three design points
and five held-out parameter/input probes, for YZ-CX and Kyriienko
two-qubit local XYZ and four-qubit Hubregtsen local Z and Z+ZZ.
Inputs are synthetic continuous values; no labels or predictive
accuracy are used. Each design selects one metric from the same
34-candidate bank at 1,024 shots and keeps it fixed at all evaluation
points and budgets. We compare isotropy, fixed diagonal anisotropy,
untuned random diagonal/dense metrics, rank-based selection and a
noise-aware variant. Both selectors prioritize rank; their tie-breakers
use, respectively, unweighted and noise-whitened retained singular
values of the distance Jacobian. All use ideal model information.

For 16 disjoint input pairs, let $J$ be the distance Jacobian and
$Q$ an orthonormal basis of parameter directions visible in the
centered reference features. Let $\nu_p$ be the exact variance of an
unbiased split-shot distance estimate at the unperturbed point.
The local diagnostic for a total displacement of $\epsilon=0.1$ is
\begin{equation}
 W=\epsilon\,\diag\bigl((2\nu_p)^{-1/2}\bigr)JQ.
 \label{eq:shotwhiten}
\end{equation}
The factor two accounts for independent estimates at the two
parameter states in the local limit. The smallest retained singular
value is reported alongside rank; the weakest sensitivity over the
\emph{whole} target space is zero if rank is deficient. Reference
spaces, directions, inputs and selected metrics are frozen before
server evaluation. This prevents a method-specific choice of favorable
test directions and does not mistake rotation of a nullspace for
an increase in total rank.

Table~\ref{tab:shotdesign} shows improvements over fixed anisotropy
for YZ-CX and Kyriienko at the level of panel medians, but not uniform
improvement at every point. The noise-aware and rank-based selectors
choose the same metric in eight of twelve panel/readout designs.
Even in the four differing designs, some held-out points deteriorate.
For Hubregtsen Z+ZZ the fourth direction becomes identifiable but
the whole-space weakest sensitivity is only $0.0052$--$0.0188$ at
4,096 requested shots: a fourth direction exists mathematically but
is weakly resolved by this local, noise-normalized diagnostic.
This statistic is neither a detection probability nor classification
accuracy. One panel's
median ratio to fixed anisotropy is below one. The result therefore
supports recovery-aware diagnosis, not generic superiority of a new
metric-learning algorithm.

All four budgets, $256$, $1{,}024$, $4{,}096$ and $16{,}384$, are
retained. Joint multinomial sampling uses 256 replicates and preserves
within-basis correlations; local XYZ splits the total across three
bases, while Z and Z+ZZ share ZZZZ outcomes. Empirical/predicted
variance ratios have pooled median $0.997$ and 5th--95th percentiles
$[0.864,1.143]$. These are descriptive calibration statistics, not
independent hypothesis tests. Method-specific ideal matched contrasts
are permitted symmetrically in the finite-displacement check, so it
remains an oracle diagnostic rather than practical detection power.
Appendix~\ref{app:shotprotocol} gives the estimator, full budget table,
measurement accounting, and cross-platform replay.

\subsection{A classical product-feature control on the same outcomes}
\label{sec:productcontrol}
This control asks a separate question: can the same ideal lifted
representation be estimated more precisely from the same outcomes?
For the Hubregtsen family in Sec.~\ref{sec:recovery}, we additionally
form every ZZ feature by multiplying the two local-Z sample means
\emph{within each independent shot half}. This changes only the
estimator, not the ideal lifted representation or its rank. Joint
Z-outcome probabilities factorize, so these products are unbiased;
the independent-half distance estimator is then unbiased as well.
Under this factorization, the product estimator is the conditional
expectation of the direct-parity estimator given the per-qubit
counts. Conditional variance therefore cannot increase for a fixed
metric and a fixed linear distance contrast. The proof and exact
covariances appear in Appendix~\ref{app:productcontrol}. This is a
classical estimation control exploiting a known model restriction,
not a new quantum measurement advantage.

\begin{corollary}[Local sensitivity under product estimation]
\label{cor:productsensitivity}
Under the product Z-distribution assumptions of
Appendix~\ref{app:productcontrol}, fix the metric, shot allocation,
parameter point, disjoint input pairs, target basis $Q$, and
displacement $\epsilon$. Use independent shot halves and independent
estimates across pairs. Suppose both estimators have strictly positive
distance variance for each pair. For $W$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:shotwhiten},
\begin{equation}
 W_{\rm product}^T W_{\rm product}
 \succeq W_{\rm direct}^T W_{\rm direct}.
 \label{eq:productinformation}
\end{equation}
Every ordered singular value is therefore nondecreasing, while the
rank and nullspace are unchanged. In particular, the smallest retained
sensitivity cannot decrease; a missing target direction remains missing.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Conditional variance gives
$0<\nu_{{\rm product},p}\le\nu_{{\rm direct},p}$ for every pair.
The ideal representation, and hence $JQ$, is identical. Writing its
$p$th row as $a_p^T$, the difference in Eq.~\eqref{eq:productinformation}
is
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\epsilon^2}{2}\sum_p
 \left(\frac{1}{\nu_{{\rm product},p}}-
       \frac{1}{\nu_{{\rm direct},p}}\right)a_pa_p^T\succeq0.
\end{equation}
The eigenvalue min--max principle gives the singular-value ordering.
Positive row weights preserve the rank and nullspace of $JQ$.
\end{proof}
This is a weak, local-limit comparison at the same fixed design.
It neither guarantees strict improvement nor compares selectors that
choose different metrics. Table~\ref{tab:shotdesign} retains the
original direct-parity sensitivity results; the paired variances below
concern finite displacements and fixed contrasts, a distinct statistic.

A supplementary local check reuses all three frozen panels, five
held-out points, four target directions, six metric arms and four
shot budgets. Both estimators use identical outcomes and the
\emph{original direct-parity contrast}; no metrics or contrasts are
retuned. At 4,096 shots, the three panel medians of the exact
product/direct contrast-variance ratio are $0.895,0.829,0.869$ for
the frozen noise-aware arm. Lower is better; these correspond to
about $10.5\%,17.1\%,13.1\%$ variance reductions for that comparison,
not hardware-shot reductions. The rank-three original-local-Z
dense-metric control remains distinct from both rank-four lifted
representations. The direct-parity draws reproduce the saved server
samples within $5.6\times10^{-14}$; the new product arm has so far
been evaluated locally, not replayed on the server. These results
reinforce the distinction between nonlinear representation and its
estimation, rather than the superiority of direct ZZ measurement.
